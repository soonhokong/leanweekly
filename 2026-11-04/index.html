AI and formalization·Nov 3

Nidhal Mghirbi and Seth Douglas give Lean-checked hyperbolic counterexamples to Kaplansky's conjectures

For every prime p, a torsion-free, word-hyperbolic group based on OpenAI's constructions fails all four group-ring conjectures in characteristic p; no torsion-free hyperbolic counterexample to any of them was previously known. Nidhal Mghirbi's Lean 4 formalization proves the main theorem, including existence forms of the random-graph estimates, and builds on OpenAI's Lean development.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2611.04599·Apsiape/kaplansky-conjectures-hyperbolic-groups

AI and formalization·Nov 4

Xi Yin, Michael R. Douglas and Anna Mei machine-check the Osterwalder-Schrader correspondence

Three Lean theorems, registered in the Palomar Registry, go from Euclidean Schwinger functions to relativistic Wightman distributions and back, in every positive spatial dimension. Euclidean growth is assumed and not recovered, so the two directions do not compose to a biconditional; field operators are not reconstructed, and four conventions are weaker than textbook ones.

⊢Checked against: palomar-registry.org·mrdouglasny/OSreconstruction-comparator

AI and formalization·Nov 4

Fedor Popov and Leonardo Rastelli release LeanVOA, a vertex operator algebra library

The Mathlib-based library (11,660 Lean files, 3,752,244 lines) constructs the Moonshine module, formalizes Zhu's modular invariance and Huang's tensor categories, and proves new results, declaring no axiom of its own. Under the authors' direction, AI agents (Claude Opus, Claude Fable, Codex) wrote the Lean code and first drafts of a 2,337-page companion book.

⊢Checked against: Fedor-Popov/Lean_VOA·leanprover.zulipchat.com

AI and formalization·Nov 4

Mahdi JafariRaviz's repository formalizes a quasipolynomial-time graph isomorphism algorithm in Lean

The proof writes the whole deterministic algorithm from a paper by MohammadTaghi Hajiaghayi and Mahdi JafariRaviz as one program for a simple machine and proves it gives correct answers within the time bound. Ten results from the literature, each relying on the classification of finite simple groups, serve as axioms.

⊢Checked against: mahdi-jfri/GraphIsomorphismLog2

AI and formalization·Nov 4

Scott Armstrong and Ahmed Bou-Rabee fully formalize Elboim-Sly paper with AI agents

Dor Elboim and Allan Sly proved Tóth's conjecture in five or more dimensions: the interchange process (particles swapping across lattice edges whose Poisson clocks ring), run long enough, yields a permutation with infinite cycles. Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Sonnet 5.5 wrote the Lean code (about 180,000 lines), which assumes nothing the paper cites.

⊢Checked against: scottnarmstrong/elboim-sly·palomar-registry.org

AI and formalization·Nov 4

Axiom Math formalizes the Stacks Project in Lean with AI agents

The library, Stacks, covers chapters 3 to 109 of the open-source algebraic geometry reference in over 15 million lines of code; Lean's kernel checks every proof, but only the StacksCore tier is human-reviewed. Axiom Math found several Stacks Project statements that do not hold in the generality stated and formalized corrected versions alongside counterexamples.

⊢Checked against: axiommath.ai·leanprover.zulipchat.com·AxiomMath/Stacks·stacks.axiommath.ai

Verification·Nov 3

Luk Koska, Tom Koska and Ben Koska verify GPU kernels in Lean

The authors formalize PTX, combining an interpreter covering 81 instruction forms with the axiomatic PTX memory model, and prove correct 9 hand-written PTX entry points (454 instructions), including simple implementations of FlashAttention and a 3-kernel MoE pipeline. LLM coding agents helped write the 34,034 lines of proof, which contain no sorry or custom axioms.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2611.04447·paperswithlean.com
