## Headlines

- **Google's Teamwork-LEAP system formalizes the Weil conjectures in Lean.** Teamwork-LEAP produced approximately 1.8 million lines of Lean code across 28,800 subagent invocations to formalize the conjectures, results about the number of solutions to polynomial equations over finite fields, originally proved by Pierre Deligne in 1974. The formalization comes from Google's Antigravity multi-agent system, which uses specialized agents powered by Gemini models. ([interestingengineering.com](https://interestingengineering.com/ai-robotics/google-antigravity-multi-agent-research))
- **Google DeepMind uses AI to verify Signal's libsignal down to machine code.** Google DeepMind released AlphaProof Code, an AI-based formal verification framework, along with the proofs. Five researchers working with an AI agent wrote about 6.5 million lines of Lean proof code in four weeks, covering a test program that uses a specific version of Rust-based libsignal, compiled into AArch64 Linux machine code. ([digitaltoday.co.kr](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/124440/deepmind-releases-alphaproof-code-to-verify-machine-code))
- **Gemini 4 agents discover formally verified quantum programs in Lean.** In the paper "Discovering formally verified quantum programs with AI", Gemini 4 agents synthesize programs against formal specifications in a Lean quantum program library and verify correctness and cost. The authors report a 290x improvement on quantum arithmetic over prior baselines, and up to 2.57x on parallel search. ([arXiv:2611.09822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09822))
- **Henrik Böving adds epr tactic for EPR, a first-order logic fragment.** Merged into Lean as #16468, the tactic uses a BitVec encoding combined with bv_decide and performs about level with Z3 on the TPTP EPR benchmark set. On verification conditions from Veil, a protocol verifier, it solves 769 (97%) of the 792 that lie fully within EPR and 1,082 (71%) of all 1,512. ([lean4#16468](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16468))
- **Codex agents prove Gonshor's 1986 conjecture on surreal birthdays in Lean.** The conjecture says a surreal product's birthday (the length of its sign sequence) is at most the natural (Hessenberg) ordinal product of the factors' birthdays. Directed by dan (@gaearon), the agents proved the CombinatorialGames library's proof_wanted statement Surreal.birthday_mul_le and a Mathlib-only version with the definitions included, checked by Comparator and independent kernel checkers. ([gaearon/gonshor-birthday](https://github.com/gaearon/gonshor-birthday))

## Verification in practice

- **&Truth says its proofs can be independently checked using Lean.** In a post on X, &Truth argues that mathematical proof offers stronger assurance by establishing that code meets precise requirements, under explicit assumptions. Its initial focus is memory safety and absence of undefined behavior. ([@andTruthPBC on X](https://x.com/andTruthPBC/status/2120922152610541730))
- **John Zhuoyang Ye and co-authors present QCCDCert, a Lean-verified trapped-ion quantum compiler.** Hardware descriptions and operating rules are compiler inputs, so one theorem covers every candidate design: compiled code preserves program behavior and obeys those rules. QCCDCert provably avoids 11 defects found in three previous compilers, and an AI agent proposing devices from checked compilation results beats the fastest fixed design on 71 of 75 benchmark programs. ([arXiv:2611.23469](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23469), [paperswithlean.com](https://paperswithlean.com/paper/2611.23469.html))
- **Bowen Zhu and Jian Wu certify plasma solver outputs with Lean proofs.** The paper reuses a machine-generated lemma from OpenAI's Lean 4 relativistic Vlasov-Maxwell library: initial fields determine the field at an outside point until plasma signals reach it. Eleven new Lean modules build on it, and the certified references exposed boundary and initialization errors in WarpX and Smilei that global energy checks missed. ([arXiv:2611.23319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23319), [paperswithlean.com](https://paperswithlean.com/paper/2611.23319.html), [tomzhu0225/vlasov-maxwell-pic-benchmark](https://github.com/tomzhu0225/vlasov-maxwell-pic-benchmark))
- **Adam Reynolds publishes Noctium, a Lean 4 framework for proving Midnight contracts.** The research preview compiles contracts written in a typed Lean DSL directly to native ZKIR circuits and Impact ledger plans, and executes them with Rust. It registers 40 active ZKIR 3.2.1 opcodes and has execution paths for C/C++, Kotlin (Android emulator tested), Swift (macOS and iOS simulator tested) and TypeScript (WASM, Chrome tested). ([awjreynolds/noctium](https://github.com/awjreynolds/noctium))
- **Smayan Agarwal and Aalok Thakkar's Kamot generates zippers, partly verified in Lean.** Kamot converts datatype declarations in a restricted Haskell fragment into zippers, which pair a focused position in a data structure with the context needed to rebuild it. Lean 4 verifies simulation, bounds, and completeness of the generated code for finite single self-recursive datatypes, using only core Lean; the rest is proved on paper. ([arXiv:2611.22572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22572), [paperswithlean.com](https://paperswithlean.com/paper/2611.22572.html), [doi.org](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24193628))

## Lean core and ecosystem

- **Leo de Moura makes equation lemmas keep `have`s instead of inlining them.** Keeping them avoids duplicating their values and preserves the join points that `do` notation introduces, which `vcgen +jp` needs to produce 2 verification conditions instead of 2ⁿ⁺¹ for `n` sequential `if`s. Sebastian Graf added `@[vcgen_rintro]`, an attribute that tells `vcgen` how to destructure a binder while it introduces the binders of a goal. ([lean4#16208](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16208), [lean4#16235](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16235), [lean4#16291](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16291))
- **Julia Himmel reimplements Lean's IO.Process.spawn using libuv.** The merged pull request fixes a number of bugs on all platforms and makes spawning much faster in processes that use a lot of memory. In a breaking change, spawn now throws an error if the command cannot be started; previously, on POSIX platforms, spawn succeeded and the child exited with code 255. ([lean4#16418](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16418))
- **Lean's CI now builds and tests Lean Beam on every Linux run.** Lean Beam (leanprover/lean-beam) lets AI agents try commands and tactics in a file's context without changing its source. Sebastian Ullrich's pull request #16447 makes PRs that break Lean Beam's build or tests fail before reaching a nightly, and unstamped PR toolchains now bundle it like releases do. ([leanprover/lean-beam](https://github.com/leanprover/lean-beam), [lean4#16447](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16447))
- **Naoya Yamashita releases leanfran, connecting Lean with Rust, Python, TypeScript and Clojure.** The tool compiles Lean functions to those languages, with proofs checked by Lean during generation and erased from the executable code; in reverse, it translates supported host-language functions to Lean for checking against Lean specifications. Its backends are tested against Lean's native C backend but have not been proved correct in Lean. ([conao3/lean-leanfran](https://github.com/conao3/lean-leanfran/), [s.conao3.com](https://s.conao3.com/blog/2026/23d62c), [leanprover.zulipchat.com](https://leanprover.zulipchat.com/#narrow/channel/583339-AI-authored-projects/topic/Leanfran.3A.20Write.20and.20prove.20functions.2C.20run.20as.20your.20language/with/641437443))
- **Naoya Yamashita's leanassay checks whether Lean theorems pin down implementations.** Its `#assay f` command breaks `f` in many small ways and reports broken versions that satisfy every theorem about `f` but change what `f` returns on an input meeting a theorem's hypotheses. The Apache-2.0 package for Lean 4.35.0 does not need the proofs, so it also works on theorems that are still `sorry`. ([conao3/lean-leanassay](https://github.com/conao3/lean-leanassay))
- **Lean 4 repository has 10,056 GitHub stars.** The leanprover/lean4 repository, home of the Lean 4 programming language and theorem prover, has 10,056 stars on GitHub. The repository was created in April 2018, and GitHub lists its language as Lean. ([leanprover/lean4](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4))
- **Leo de Moura speeds up equation generation for `match` on many literals.** With 64 literal patterns, generating equation theorems (which `simp [f]` and `cbv` need) used to hit the heartbeat limit. Leo de Moura fixed this with a series of pull requests; one makes equation generation for a `String → Nat` function with 128 literal alternatives and a catch-all about 20% faster. ([lean4#16354](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16354), [lean4#16460](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16460), [lean4#16465](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16465), [lean4#16477](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16477), [lean4#16480](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16480), [lean4#16502](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16502), [lean4#16505](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16505))
- **Mac Malone lets Lake packages declare their own cache services.** Services are specified in a `cache_service` TOML table or Lean DSL declaration and can be used via `lake cache get --service`. A new `downloadCacheService` package option sets the service Lake uses by default for `lake cache get` of the package and, when the artifact cache is enabled, for on-demand downloads during builds. ([lean4#16302](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16302), [lean4#16351](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16351))
- **Sebastian Ullrich speeds up deriving Repr, BEq, and Hashable on mutual blocks.** Lean now derives these classes once per mutual block, generating the auxiliary functions once for the whole block instead of once per type. This removes quadratic elaboration cost: deriving Repr on every type of a 76-type mutual block went from 309 seconds and 10.2 GB to 62 seconds and 1.3 GB. ([lean4#16339](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16339))

## Papers, events, press

- **Leo de Moura presents "Scaling Lean for AI" at SViL, Stanford.** The November 7, 2026 slide deck by the Lean FRO's chief architect covers AI-written proofs, including Claude's Lean proof of Fermat's Last Theorem, and verification projects such as AWS's Cedar, Microsoft's SymCrypt, and Google's Kraken. It concludes that software verification is now a major use case and scalability the new challenge. ([leodemoura.github.io](https://leodemoura.github.io/static/svil-palo-alto-2026/))
- **Xueying Qin, Marco Peressotti and Fabrizio Montesi mechanise asynchronous choreographies in Lean.** The paper presents Nyarlathotep, the first asynchronous nondeterministic choreographic programming framework, and proves that endpoint projection, which compiles a global program into network programs, is sound and complete and yields deadlock-free networks. The Lean 4 mechanisation takes about 20,000 lines using CSLib, compared with about 40,000 for Mech, the less featureful calculus Nyarlathotep extends. ([arXiv:2611.24649](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24649), [paperswithlean.com](https://paperswithlean.com/paper/2611.24649.html))
