Google's Teamwork-LEAP system formalizes the Weil conjectures in Lean

Teamwork-LEAP produced approximately 1.8 million lines of Lean code across 28,800 subagent invocations to formalize the conjectures, results about the number of solutions to polynomial equations over finite fields, originally proved by Pierre Deligne in 1974. The formalization comes from Google's Antigravity multi-agent system, which uses specialized agents powered by Gemini models.

Lean core·Nov 16

Henrik Böving adds epr tactic for EPR, a first-order logic fragment

Merged into Lean as #16468, the tactic uses a BitVec encoding combined with bv_decide and performs about level with Z3 on the TPTP EPR benchmark set. On verification conditions from Veil, a protocol verifier, it solves 769 (97%) of the 792 that lie fully within EPR and 1,082 (71%) of all 1,512.

⊢Checked against: lean4#16468

AI and formalization·Nov 19

Codex agents prove Gonshor's 1986 conjecture on surreal birthdays in Lean

The conjecture says a surreal product's birthday (the length of its sign sequence) is at most the natural (Hessenberg) ordinal product of the factors' birthdays. Directed by dan (@gaearon), the agents proved the CombinatorialGames library's proof_wanted statement Surreal.birthday_mul_le and a Mathlib-only version with the definitions included, checked by Comparator and independent kernel checkers.

⊢Checked against: gaearon/gonshor-birthday

Verification·Nov 17

John Zhuoyang Ye and co-authors present QCCDCert, a Lean-verified trapped-ion quantum compiler

Hardware descriptions and operating rules are compiler inputs, so one theorem covers every candidate design: compiled code preserves program behavior and obeys those rules. QCCDCert provably avoids 11 defects found in three previous compilers, and an AI agent proposing devices from checked compilation results beats the fastest fixed design on 71 of 75 benchmark programs.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2611.23469·paperswithlean.com

Lean core·Nov 7

Sebastian Ullrich speeds up deriving Repr, BEq, and Hashable on mutual blocks

Lean now derives these classes once per mutual block, generating the auxiliary functions once for the whole block instead of once per type. This removes quadratic elaboration cost: deriving Repr on every type of a 76-type mutual block went from 309 seconds and 10.2 GB to 62 seconds and 1.3 GB.

⊢Checked against: lean4#16339

Papers and events·Nov 18

Xueying Qin, Marco Peressotti and Fabrizio Montesi mechanise asynchronous choreographies in Lean

The paper presents Nyarlathotep, the first asynchronous nondeterministic choreographic programming framework, and proves that endpoint projection, which compiles a global program into network programs, is sound and complete and yields deadlock-free networks. The Lean 4 mechanisation takes about 20,000 lines using CSLib, compared with about 40,000 for Mech, the less featureful calculus Nyarlathotep extends.

Teng Zhang proves Schmeisser's 1977 conjecture; OpenAI's Codex formalizes it in Lean

The conjecture strengthens Sendov's: if a polynomial's zeros lie in the closed unit disk, every point of their convex hull is within distance 1 of some critical point. The Mathlib-based formalization covers every degree with no sorry placeholders or extra axioms, and Lean's kernel checks its interval certificates for degrees 4 to 6,000.

Lean harness ForShor verifies Shor's algorithm, including its asymptotic gate cost

Anirudh Suresh, Joshua M. Courtney, Runzhou Tao, and Yudong Cao prove correctness and gate-count theorems once for all valid policies (Toom-Cook multiplication designs, possibly from AI agents) and publish an open challenge at forshor.ai. The port from Rocq took about six weeks, covers typed CompCert C through x86-64 assembly, and discharges all 4,908 proof obligations without project-specific axioms; 88.8% of 1,071 human interactions concerned workflow and coordination.
