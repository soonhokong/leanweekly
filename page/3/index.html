Verification·Nov 3

Jesse Comer and coauthors present Whiel for Lean-certified relational program verification

Whiel verifies programs whose states are database instances against Hoare specifications: an LLM proposes loop invariants, and the Vampire first-order theorem prover checks their verification conditions. An independent certifier constructs Lean proofs of the original specification or its negation; the 86 benchmark tasks have certified reference answers, 69 valid and 17 invalid.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2611.05612·JesseComer/whiel

The Lean 4 library and CLI (Apache-2.0 license) compute periods, deadlines, ages, holidays, and taxes under Japanese law, including income and inheritance tax. Lean core·Nov 1

Leo de Moura cuts Mathlib build instruction counts, speeds up rw tactic

Three interpreter and Meta changes cut Mathlib build instructions by 1.8% (a cache for callee lookups), 3.7% (in-place evaluation of USize, UInt64 and Array.uget/uset primitives) and 2.7% (a cache of definition unfoldings). Two others make rw faster on large goals and on goals with shared subterms.

⊢Checked against: lean4#16153·lean4#16148·lean4#16155·lean4#16130·lean4#16131

Lean core·Oct 30

Leo de Moura fixes three quadratic performance issues in Lean

The issues include an Expr hash collision: chains of the same application with different lengths could share hashes, so kernel cache lookups compared them structurally and checks became quadratic. The other two are quadratic elaboration of large string-literal equalities and quadratic compilation of large list literals.

⊢Checked against: lean4#16107·lean4#16046·lean4#16049

Lean core·Oct 29

Sebastian Graf lets Lean's vcgen tactic discharge frame conditions with frameproc theorems

The vcgen tactic now discharges the frame condition of each framed call with a theorem named by a @[frameproc], for example one for a separation logic, so the frame condition no longer becomes a verification condition. Sebastian Graf also added the @[vcgen_simp] attribute, which collects Sym.simp theorems for vcgen.

⊢Checked against: lean4#16093·lean4#15955

Researchers formalize the Strong Perfect Graph Theorem in Lean with AI agents

The complete Lean proof follows, result by result, the 2006 proof by Maria Chudnovsky, Neil Robertson, Paul Seymour and Robin Thomas that a finite graph is perfect (every induced subgraph's chromatic number equals its clique number) exactly when it is Berge (it and its complement lack induced odd cycles of length at least five).

AI and formalization·Nov 4

Xi Yin, Michael R. Douglas and Anna Mei machine-check the Osterwalder-Schrader correspondence

Three Lean theorems, registered in the Palomar Registry, go from Euclidean Schwinger functions to relativistic Wightman distributions and back, in every positive spatial dimension. Euclidean growth is assumed and not recovered, so the two directions do not compose to a biconditional; field operators are not reconstructed, and four conventions are weaker than textbook ones.

⊢Checked against: palomar-registry.org·mrdouglasny/OSreconstruction-comparator

AI and formalization·Nov 3

Nidhal Mghirbi and Seth Douglas give Lean-checked hyperbolic counterexamples to Kaplansky's conjectures

For every prime p, a torsion-free, word-hyperbolic group based on OpenAI's constructions fails all four group-ring conjectures in characteristic p; no torsion-free hyperbolic counterexample to any of them was previously known. Nidhal Mghirbi's Lean 4 formalization proves the main theorem, including existence forms of the random-graph estimates, and builds on OpenAI's Lean development.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2611.04599·Apsiape/kaplansky-conjectures-hyperbolic-groups

Verification·Nov 2

Sreehari Sankar and coauthors present LeanGuard, Lean-verified guardrails for AI agents

LeanGuard checks every tool call an agent proposes against a Lean 4 policy, using a decision procedure proved equivalent to a LeanLTL temporal semantics. On Tau-Bench, unguarded Qwen agents executed at least 33 policy-forbidden calls while LeanGuard executed none, and it cut direct prompt-injection success on DTap from 57.9% to 5.9%.

The paper introduces blazing-smtlib, a Rust parsing library for SMT-LIB (a standard input format for SMT solvers), and its Lean port BSmtLib, whose optimised lexer and parser provably match a formalised SMT-LIB syntax. Sébastien Celles uses Lean 4 to find correctness bugs in Go

Inspired by julia-in-lean, the go-in-lean proof-of-concept models a bounded subset of Go (including all of math/bits), states each function's documented behaviour as a theorem, proves it with Lean's kernel and bv_decide, and turns failed proofs into Go tests. Papers and events·Oct 30

Darius Foo and coauthors reconstruct the Iris base logic in Lean

Indirection Theory is simpler than Iris, thanks to ordinary dependent types, but less expressive; the authors' lightweight Lean mechanisation generalises it to Iris-style resources. Two case studies, including a program logic complete enough to verify concurrent and higher-order programs, show the base logic is expressive and faithful to Iris.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2610.41202

AI and formalization·Nov 1

Zayn Blore proves in Lean that the Poisson conjecture is false

Following the explicit counterexample in Christopher D. Long's unrefereed 2026 preprint, the proof shows the conjecture (every substitution preserving the canonical Poisson bracket is invertible) fails for two canonical pairs and hence for every number of pairs from two up. It proves four statements from Google DeepMind's Formal Conjectures, with their definitions copied verbatim.

⊢Checked against: zblore/fc-poisson-2·palomar-registry.org
