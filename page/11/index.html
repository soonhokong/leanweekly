Verification·Jan 21

Ilya Sergey shows how to verify imperative Lean 4 programs with Velvet

A Proofs and Intuitions post walks through Velvet, a verifier embedded in Lean as a library and developed by VERSE Lab. Velvet discharges most proof obligations with SMT solvers (such as Z3 and cvc5) and tactics like grind, then leaves the rest for interactive proof. In the example, Claude Code and Harmonic's Aristotle each finished the remaining proof.

⊢Checked against: proofsandintuitions.net

AI and formalization·Jan 20

Project Numina announces Numina-Lean-Agent, an open-source theorem-proving framework

Yann Fleureau announced Numina-Lean-Agent, an open-source agentic framework for autoformalization and theorem proving using generic AI models. The announcement reports 12/12 on Putnam 2025 and a formalization of the "Effective Brascamp-Lieb inequalities" research paper, more than 90% of it AI-generated.

AI and formalization·Jan 11

Axiom's AxiomProver solves all 12 Putnam problems with Lean proofs

AxiomProver, an autonomous AI theorem prover built by Axiom, solved 12 out of 12 problems from the Putnam Competition, 8 of them within the strict exam time limit, producing formal proofs in Lean. One combinatorial step in Problem A5 required 2,054 lines of formal proof.

⊢Checked against: LinkedIn post

Lean core·Jan 10

Sebastian Ullrich proposes bundling Lean's Init library into one .olean file

A pull request by Sebastian Ullrich bundles all Init module files into a single file with cross-sharing, reducing cumulative size from 325MB to 264MB and openat calls for import Init on Linux from about 2000 to about 80. The pull request is still open.

⊢Checked against: lean4#11965

AI and formalization·Jan 9

Hacker News discusses Erdos problem #728 solved largely autonomously by AI

A Hacker News thread on the claim that Erdos problem #728 "was solved more or less autonomously by AI" drew 619 points and 363 comments. A Harmonic employee said Aristotle, the company's system, can translate an informal English proof into Lean, and commenters debated how to check that a theorem statement is formalized correctly.

Lean Community holds Lean Together 2026 virtually via Zoom, January 19 to 23

Lean Together is an annual meeting for users, developers, and fans of the Lean programming language and theorem prover, covering formalized mathematics, software verification, infrastructure, and outreach. The meeting is open to everyone without cost, and talks will be recorded with speakers' permission and posted to the leanprover community YouTube channel.

Lean core·Dec 30

Leo de Moura adds benchmarks showing SymM beating MetaM on intros/apply

A merged Lean 4 pull request by Leo de Moura adds performance comparison tests between the new SymM monad and the standard MetaM for intros/apply operations. Results show a 10-20x speedup, rising from 10.8x at size 1000 to 22.0x at size 6000.

⊢Checked against: lean4#11838·leanprover/lean4
