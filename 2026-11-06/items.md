## Headlines

- **Zayn Blore machine-checks the Dixmier conjecture's failure in dimension 3 and above.** The Palomar registry entry proves in Lean that, over any field of characteristic zero and in each dimension, the Dixmier conjecture implies the Jacobian conjecture, and checks an explicit counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture in every dimension of 3 or more. Its four statements are copied verbatim from Google DeepMind's Formal Conjectures. ([palomar-registry.org](https://palomar-registry.org/entry?id=PALOMAR-2026-11-06-000008&version=1), [zblore/fc-dixmier-jacobian](https://github.com/zblore/fc-dixmier-jacobian))
- **Anthropic releases FlashProve, with Lean proofs of its security properties.** Anthropic released FlashProve, which uses a zkSNARK (a type of cryptographic proof) to prove which AI model answered a request. Anthropic used Lean to specify FlashProve's protocol and prove completeness (honest proofs are accepted), soundness (fake proofs are rejected) and zero-knowledge (proofs hide the model's weights), and shipped an executable, provably correct Lean verifier. ([anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com/research/provable-inference), [anthropics/FlashProve](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve))
- **Kiro proves properties of its own agent harness in Lean.** Kiro wrote Lean models of 26 functions from its TypeScript, Rust, and Python harness (the code managing context, permissions, and state), proving 70 of 96 properties; 18 falsified ones reproduced on the real code. “Lean’s kernel checks every proof, the real code checks every finding,” says the post, which Swami Sivasubramanian shared on LinkedIn. ([kiro.dev](https://kiro.dev/blog/harness-properties-in-lean/), [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/swaminathansivasubramanian_automated-reasoning-has-been-a-core-investment-share-7523502466101886976-ZBJB/))
- **Thanin Quartz and coauthors prove 2×2 matrix mortality undecidable, checked in Lean.** Matrix mortality asks whether some product of matrices from a finite family equals the zero matrix; only dimension two was open, with higher dimensions known undecidable since 1970. The result holds for families of 5,799 distinct 2×2 integer matrices, and a Lean 4 formalization checks the full reduction from halting, without project-specific axioms. ([arXiv:2611.06562](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06562), [paperswithlean.com](https://paperswithlean.com/paper/2611.06562.html))
- **Alpha Squared Labs formalizes the Atiyah–Singer index theorem in Lean 4.** The project also covers the theorem's cohomological and twisted spin Dirac formulations, and Comparator, a harness checking seven independently written propositions, reported acceptance by both Lean's default kernel and the Nanoda kernel. AI assisted with Lean statements and proof development under human maintainers' responsibility; no new mathematical theorem is claimed. ([alpha-squared-labs/Lean-Atiyah-Singer](https://github.com/alpha-squared-labs/Lean-Atiyah-Singer))

## Verification in practice

- **Jesse Comer and coauthors present Whiel for Lean-certified relational program verification.** Whiel verifies programs whose states are database instances against Hoare specifications: an LLM proposes loop invariants, and the Vampire first-order theorem prover checks their verification conditions. An independent certifier constructs Lean proofs of the original specification or its negation; the 86 benchmark tasks have certified reference answers, 69 valid and 17 invalid. ([arXiv:2611.05612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05612), [JesseComer/whiel](https://github.com/JesseComer/whiel))
- **Naoya Yamashita releases lean-ritsuryo, Japanese statutory computation with proofs in Lean.** The Lean 4 library and CLI (Apache-2.0 license) compute periods, deadlines, ages, holidays, and taxes under Japanese law, including income and inheritance tax. The library's functions come with proofs that they compute what the provisions say, and the CLI computes by calling them; the README also links a paper. ([conao3/lean-ritsuryo](https://github.com/conao3/lean-ritsuryo))

## Lean core and ecosystem

- **Leo de Moura cuts Mathlib build instruction counts, speeds up rw tactic.** Three interpreter and Meta changes cut Mathlib build instructions by 1.8% (a cache for callee lookups), 3.7% (in-place evaluation of USize, UInt64 and Array.uget/uset primitives) and 2.7% (a cache of definition unfoldings). Two others make rw faster on large goals and on goals with shared subterms. ([lean4#16153](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16153), [lean4#16148](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16148), [lean4#16155](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16155), [lean4#16130](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16130), [lean4#16131](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16131))
- **Leo de Moura fixes three quadratic performance issues in Lean.** The issues include an Expr hash collision: chains of the same application with different lengths could share hashes, so kernel cache lookups compared them structurally and checks became quadratic. The other two are quadratic elaboration of large string-literal equalities and quadratic compilation of large list literals. ([lean4#16107](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16107), [lean4#16046](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16046), [lean4#16049](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16049))
- **Sebastian Graf lets Lean's vcgen tactic discharge frame conditions with frameproc theorems.** The vcgen tactic now discharges the frame condition of each framed call with a theorem named by a `@[frameproc]`, for example one for a separation logic, so the frame condition no longer becomes a verification condition. Sebastian Graf also added the `@[vcgen_simp]` attribute, which collects `Sym.simp` theorems for vcgen. ([lean4#16093](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/16093), [lean4#15955](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/15955))
