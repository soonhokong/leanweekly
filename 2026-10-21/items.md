## Headlines

- **Matija Bucić, Fankang He and Hyunwoo Lee prove Erdős-Szemerédi sunflower conjecture.** Posed in the 1970s, the conjecture bounds the size of any family of subsets containing no k-sunflower and was previously known only for k = 3, via cap-set results. According to paperswithlean.com, the proof was machine-verified in Lean 4, with the formalization to be released in the Palomar repository. ([arXiv:2610.26686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26686), [paperswithlean.com](https://paperswithlean.com/paper/2610.26686.html))
- **Thomas Browning and coauthors release AnnalsChallenge, 50 Annals theorems stated in Lean.** Each statement, written by hand in Lean 4 using Mathlib, is the main result of an Annals of Mathematics paper published since 2020; all were manually reviewed, then audited by AI, and are released without formalized proofs. The authors say measuring progress in AI autoformalization of mathematics requires hard, trustworthy datasets of formal statements. ([arXiv:2610.18301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18301))
- **AI agents help researchers discover a new consensus protocol, verified in Lean.** AI-greement, an agent harness with a Lean-based verification framework, helped researchers discover A-greement, a consensus protocol proved linearizable in about 98,000 lines of Lean. A-greement cuts mean latency over the fastest baseline by 12.2% on write-heavy and 33.3% on read-heavy workloads, and the harness's Lean formalization uncovered four previously unreported vulnerabilities in existing protocols. ([arXiv:2610.30114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30114), [paperswithlean.com](https://paperswithlean.com/paper/2610.30114.html))

## AI and formalization

- **Anthropic's Claude designs more efficient quantum circuits, verifying one with Lean proofs.** Working largely on its own, Claude Mythos 5 produced circuits that improve on the best published versions. For a new circuit computing modular inversion, the wraparound version of division, Claude checked the formula behind the design with large-scale numerical simulations, then formalized the claim in Lean, a step Anthropic attributes to the model's caution. ([anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com/research/designing-efficient-quantum-circuits), [arXiv:2610.22367](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22367))
- **SAIR launches Open Math OS, a research workspace built on open models.** Open Math OS offers open-source models and AI agents that explore problems in parallel, and gives every project a 24/7 cloud machine with more than 100 mathematical and scientific computing tools preinstalled. Compute is free for approved teams, the mathematical community owns its data, and launch partners include Hugging Face, Lean, CSCS and EPFL. ([openmathmodel.org](https://www.openmathmodel.org/), [@SAIRfoundation on X](https://x.com/SAIRfoundation/status/2111106860468285931), [@SAIRfoundation on X](https://x.com/SAIRfoundation/status/2111106862120833055))

## Verification in practice

- **Kraken Lean project proves FFmpeg's SBR decoder binary safe.** The theorem sbr_session_safe covers the linked machine code (aacsbr.o with its callees): given the link's layout, external routines' contracts and a 32-bit bound on their stack, every run stays in the memory it owns and uses at most the stated stack. The prefix-code and bound facts of FFmpeg's Huffman tables are proved. ([zrlk/kraken](https://github.com/zrlk/kraken/blob/sbr-wf-preserve/Kraken/X64/Examples/Sbr/SessionBin/Top.lean))
- **Ilya Sergey describes Vermilion, an experimental Lean backend for Rust verifier Verus.** Vermilion expresses Verus's verification conditions (mathematical propositions about a program), normally sent to the Z3 solver, as Lean theorems, though it does not prove that they correctly describe Rust's semantics. Its default tactic, vrml, tries to prove each automatically, and any it leaves open can be proved interactively, drawing on Mathlib. ([proofsandintuitions.net](https://proofsandintuitions.net/2026/10/18/vermilion-experiment-lean-backend-verus/), [@ilyasergey on X](https://x.com/ilyasergey/status/2111708968662036516))
- **Vasily Ilin shares a prompt to build verified, faster software in Lean.** The prompt asks for a Lean version of software X, proved correct and optimized to run at least 10% faster than X's fastest implementation, with tests and proofs passing throughout. Kim Morrison's lean-zip, a verified DEFLATE implementation, inspired the workflow; examples cover integer factorization and jump processes, the latter faster than Julia's JumpProcesses.jl. ([@IlinVasily29521 on X](https://x.com/IlinVasily29521/status/2110973208799367198), [Vilin97/PrimeFactorLean](https://github.com/Vilin97/PrimeFactorLean), [Vilin97/JumpProcessesLean](https://github.com/Vilin97/JumpProcessesLean), [kim-em/lean-zip](https://github.com/kim-em/lean-zip))

## Lean core and ecosystem

- **Linux 7.3 works around AMD CPU bug behind Lean build segfaults.** The FRO noticed probabilistic segfaults while compiling big Lean projects on certain AMD CPUs running Linux 6.15 or later, and reported them to the Linux kernel. Users of AMD Zen 5 CPUs on kernels 6.15 through 7.2 should consider kernel 7.3 and/or the latest AMD microcode, or, failing that, boot with the option tlbi=ipi. ([leanprover.zulipchat.com](https://leanprover.zulipchat.com/#narrow/channel/113486-General-announcements/topic/Linux.207.2E3.20contains.20a.20bugfix.20for.20Lean/with/633073219), [lore.kernel.org](https://lore.kernel.org/lkml/CAAuFnRTyva1_3tsF3vrMBL+TLS1YL4EgUPT2c3O9k7A9hWUMnA@mail.gmail.com/))
