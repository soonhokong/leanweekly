Verification·Nov 16

Lean harness ForShor verifies Shor's algorithm, including its asymptotic gate cost

Anirudh Suresh, Joshua M. Courtney, Runzhou Tao, and Yudong Cao prove correctness and gate-count theorems once for all valid policies (Toom-Cook multiplication designs, possibly from AI agents) and publish an open challenge at forshor.ai. At 2048 bits, a varying-arity policy gives about 1.685 trillion T-gates, against 1.820 trillion for the best single arity.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2611.20342·VerifiedQC/ForShor·paperswithlean.com

Verification·Nov 16

QECLean, a Lean framework, proves an IBM quantum code has distance 24

Stavan Jain, Nithin Raveendran and Aws Albarghouthi's framework, built on Mathlib, backs every claimed property of quantum error-correcting codes with a machine-checked proof. Its new code combinators prove distance compositionally for large codes beyond algorithmic techniques (24 was previously only an upper bound), and it formalizes entire code families such as the surface code.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2611.21236·QECLean/QECLean

Verification·Nov 14

Rylan Malarchick presents qlin, a dynamic quantum circuit compiler with Lean proofs

Lean 4 proofs show that qlin moves operations out of measurement-dependent branches without delaying the program, and that its check of each compiled program against its source is sound. Modeled at the QICK controller's 42 ns per decision, 10 of 65 programs run at least 10% faster than the best baseline, up to 91.2%.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2611.19618·rylanmalarchick/qlin

Verification·Nov 14

TrustBound proves in Lean how to account for payments outliving AI agents

Sandip Nandi, Aishwarya Bose, Apurba Bose and Soovadeep Bakshi prove that once a fence stops new admissions, discharging outstanding rights prevents further payments, and that truthful outcomes give exact accounting. Lean accepts six histories from a Python/SQLite service; concurrent tests exposed a conflict from reordered provider reads, fixed in all 84 tested cases.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2611.19551·tokencraftdata/trustbound·paperswithlean.com

Verification·Nov 16

NyxFoundation publishes LeanAsm, a Lean 4 foundation for verified RV64 assembly

LeanAsm covers typed RV64IM (RISC-V) and scalar x86-64 assembly, executable machine semantics, encoding, and source emission; it is intended to be shared by ssz-asm, cl-asm, and p2p-asm. Its architecture follows the verified macro-assembly approach demonstrated by Verified-zkEVM's evm-asm while remaining independent of EVM, zkVM execution, and riscv-zkvm.

Verification·Nov 13

Nethermind's Foxglove formalizes a separation logic for probabilistic programs in Lean

Built on iris-lean, Foxglove adapts the Bluebell logic of Bao, D'Osualdo and Farzan to functional programs and is intended to support verifying cryptographic primitives formalized in ArkLib. Every rule of the logic is proved against a semantic model with no axioms beyond Lean's own, and the Ethereum Foundation funded the work.

Verification·Nov 10

AI-written Lean model of CPython 3.15.2 finds hundreds of bugs

David Andrews and Chao Zhang's model spans 613,318 lines of Lean with 18,898 theorems and lemmas; each modelled function must agree with the real interpreter on at least 50,000 cases. Of 26,004 functions in scope, 4,071 (15.7%) are judged faithful by independent review, and 418 bug records reproduce on 3.15.2, each with a reproducer.

⊢Checked against: Broyojo/python-formalization

Keel, an in-kernel proof checker for the Linux eBPF verifier, is specified in Lean and proved sound against bit-vector semantics; its implementation in EmbC, a deeply embedded C fragment, is proved to refine that specification.
