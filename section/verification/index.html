Verification·Nov 13

Daksh Shami and Charles Yuan verify MARBLE's rewrite rules in Lean 4

The MARBLE language compiles publication-style matrix formulas into quantum circuits, applying algebraic rewrite rules that decompose each matrix into simpler matrices with known circuits; Lean 4 proofs show every rule preserves matrix semantics. MARBLE matches or surpasses 37 of 39 published baselines and produces exponentially smaller circuits than the leading tool FABLE.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2611.17756·paperswithlean.com

⊢Checked against: digitaltoday.co.kr

Verification·Nov 17

John Zhuoyang Ye and co-authors present QCCDCert, a Lean-verified trapped-ion quantum compiler

Hardware descriptions and operating rules are compiler inputs, so one theorem covers every candidate design: compiled code preserves program behavior and obeys those rules. QCCDCert provably avoids 11 defects found in three previous compilers, and an AI agent proposing devices from checked compilation results beats the fastest fixed design on 71 of 75 benchmark programs.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2611.23469·paperswithlean.com

Verification·Nov 15

Bowen Zhu and Jian Wu certify plasma solver outputs with Lean proofs

The paper reuses a machine-generated lemma from OpenAI's Lean 4 relativistic Vlasov-Maxwell library: initial fields determine the field at an outside point until plasma signals reach it. Eleven new Lean modules build on it, and the certified references exposed boundary and initialization errors in WarpX and Smilei that global energy checks missed.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2611.23319·paperswithlean.com·tomzhu0225/vlasov-maxwell-pic-benchmark

Verification·Nov 16

Lean harness ForShor verifies Shor's algorithm, including its asymptotic gate cost

Anirudh Suresh, Joshua M. Courtney, Runzhou Tao, and Yudong Cao prove correctness and gate-count theorems once for all valid policies (Toom-Cook multiplication designs, possibly from AI agents) and publish an open challenge at forshor.ai. Verification·Nov 14

Rylan Malarchick presents qlin, a dynamic quantum circuit compiler with Lean proofs

Lean 4 proofs show that qlin moves operations out of measurement-dependent branches without delaying the program, and that its check of each compiled program against its source is sound. Modeled at the QICK controller's 42 ns per decision, 10 of 65 programs run at least 10% faster than the best baseline, up to 91.2%.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2611.19618·rylanmalarchick/qlin

Verification·Nov 14

TrustBound proves in Lean how to account for payments outliving AI agents

Sandip Nandi, Aishwarya Bose, Apurba Bose and Soovadeep Bakshi prove that once a fence stops new admissions, discharging outstanding rights prevents further payments, and that truthful outcomes give exact accounting. Lean accepts six histories from a Python/SQLite service; concurrent tests exposed a conflict from reordered provider reads, fixed in all 84 tested cases.

Verification·Nov 16

The open-source toolkit translates supported Zig functions into executable Lean models, working from Zig's analyzed intermediate representation (AIR); an included copy-loop example proves its model avoids invalid accesses and uninitialized reads. LeanAsm covers typed RV64IM (RISC-V) and scalar x86-64 assembly, executable machine semantics, encoding, and source emission; it is intended to be shared by ssz-asm, cl-asm, and p2p-asm.
