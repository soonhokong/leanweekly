Papers and events·Feb 16

Stephen Diehl tests Opus 4.6 on a category theory library in Lean

Stephen Diehl built Kitty Cats, about 900 lines of Lean, with Anthropic's Opus 4.6 over two 30-minute sessions. The model handled the Yoneda lemma but needed guidance on multi-step rewrites and on refactoring verbose proofs. Diehl credits the lean-lsp-mcp server and the lean4-skills plugin for giving the model a feedback loop.

Papers and events·Feb 2

Blog post formally models a Cyberchase episode's dragon game in Lean

A post on shadaj.me, "Formally Verifying PBS Kids with Lean4," uses Lean to model the Cyberchase episode "Problem Solving in Shangri-La," a variant of Nim where players remove 1, 2, or 3 dragons. It proves the Cybersquad's poison-number strategy wins, introducing tactics such as rw, simp, split, and omega along the way.

Papers and events·Jan 29

Stan Fedotov compares two AI-assisted math papers, one checked in Lean

In a LinkedIn post, Stan Fedotov argues the field is "moving towards automated verification in most domains," while noting Lean is "only useful when the standard library supports your field." In one paper, only a somewhat elementary part of the proof was verified in Lean, while the authors proved the deeper parts.

Leo de Moura presents The State of Lean at Lean Together 2026

Leo de Moura, Chief Architect of the Lean FRO, reviewed 2025 at Lean Together in January 2026: 12 releases, a new compiler fully implemented in Lean, and the grind proof-automation tactic. The talk also said 2026 will be the last year with significant changes to the language and standard library.

Papers and events·Jan 12

Martin Ciupa summarizes Tom Kalil's interview with Terence Tao on AI in math

In a LinkedIn post, Martin Ciupa recaps the conversation, in which Terence Tao calls automating formalization (turning ordinary math into proof-assistant-checked form) the near-term change, stresses formal verification over trusting AI blindly, and highlights Lean-style autocomplete that fills a line or two at a time.
