AI and formalization·Nov 7

Murali Menon proves Herzog-Schönheim conjecture for solvable groups, with Lean formalization

The Herzog-Schönheim conjecture states that if a group is partitioned into finitely many, and at least two, cosets of subgroups, then two of these subgroups have the same index. The arXiv paper proves it for all solvable groups, finite or infinite, and the proof has been formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib.

Thanin Quartz and coauthors prove 2×2 matrix mortality undecidable, checked in Lean

Matrix mortality asks whether some product of matrices from a finite family equals the zero matrix; only dimension two was open, with higher dimensions known undecidable since 1970. The result holds for families of 5,799 distinct 2×2 integer matrices, and a Lean 4 formalization checks the full reduction from halting, without project-specific axioms.

The complete Lean proof follows, result by result, the 2006 proof by Maria Chudnovsky, Neil Robertson, Paul Seymour and Robin Thomas that a finite graph is perfect (every induced subgraph's chromatic number equals its clique number) exactly when it is Berge (it and its complement lack induced odd cycles of length at least five).

AI and formalization·Nov 4

Xi Yin, Michael R. Douglas and Anna Mei machine-check the Osterwalder-Schrader correspondence

Three Lean theorems, registered in the Palomar Registry, go from Euclidean Schwinger functions to relativistic Wightman distributions and back, in every positive spatial dimension. For every prime p, a torsion-free, word-hyperbolic group based on OpenAI's constructions fails all four group-ring conjectures in characteristic p; no torsion-free hyperbolic counterexample to any of them was previously known.
