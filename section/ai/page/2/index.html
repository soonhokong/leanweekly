AI and formalization·May 28

The Mathlib Initiative launches Formal Frontier for open-source AI autoformalization

Formal Frontier is a new project focused on responsible, scalable, and open-source AI-driven autoformalization of mathematics, with XTX Markets supporting it. Its initial phase covers a community autoformalization specification, open-source tooling, and autoformalized texts chosen to fill gaps in Mathlib's existing coverage.

⊢Checked against: mathlib-initiative.org

AI and formalization·Apr 25

Sidharth Hariharan, Maryna Viazovska and coauthors report on formalizing 8-dimensional sphere packing

The paper describes how Maryna Viazovska's 2016 solution of the sphere packing problem in dimension 8 was formally verified in Lean in February 2026, with the final stages done by Math, Inc.'s autoformalization model Gauss. It discusses the techniques used, the collaboration between humans and Gauss, and remaining project objectives.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2604.23468

AI and formalization·Apr 23

Alex Meiburg shares Aristotle-authored Z80 emulator with verified assembly GCD

Alex Meiburg asked Aristotle to formalize a Z80 emulator, build an assembler, and write a BigNum GCD in about 80 assembly instructions, then verify it by modeling the whole CPU execution. After 14 rounds of submitting to Aristotle (without editing the prompt), the proof succeeded.

⊢Checked against: leanprover.zulipchat.com

AI and formalization·Apr 21

New arXiv papers test LLM formalization faithfulness, agents, and benchmarks in Lean

Kyuhee Kim, Auguste Poiroux and Antoine Bosselut evaluate GPT-5 and DeepSeek-R1 on 303 first-order logic problems: GPT-5 fabricates axioms during proof generation, while DeepSeek-R1 mistranslates premises. Other papers introduce PRIME, a benchmark of 156 theorems annotated in Lean 4, analyze tool-augmented formalization agents, and formalize the Wu-Ritt characteristic set method.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2604.19459·arXiv:2604.19000·arXiv:2604.16538·arXiv:2604.14912·+1 more

AI and formalization·Mar 16

Mistral AI releases Leanstral, an open-source code agent for Lean 4

Mistral AI released Leanstral, with 6B active parameters and weights under an Apache 2.0 license. On FLTEval, a new suite built from pull requests to the FLT project, Leanstral pass@2 scores 26.3, beating Sonnet by 2.6 points while costing $36 to run versus Sonnet's $549.

⊢Checked against: mistral.ai

AI and formalization·Mar 9

Joseph Tooby-Smith's Lean formalization finds error in widely cited 2006 physics paper

Formalizing a 2006 paper on the stability of the two Higgs doublet model (2HDM) potential, using Mathlib and Physlib, revealed an error invalidating its main theorem. The paper calls this, to the best of its knowledge, the first non-trivial error in a physics paper found through formalization.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2603.08139

AI and formalization·Mar 5

Math, Inc.'s Gauss completes formal proof of sphere packing in dimensions 8 and 24

Math, Inc. used Gauss to help formally verify Maryna Viazovska's Fields Medal-winning sphere packing results, proving all remaining 8-dimensional results in 5 days and autoformalizing the 24-dimensional case in two weeks. The formalization grew from 70k to about 200k lines.

⊢Checked against: math.inc

AI and formalization·Mar 2

Math, Inc.'s Gauss autoformalizes Viazovska's 24-dimensional sphere-packing proof in Lean

Gauss, a Math, Inc. reasoning agent built to formalize proofs automatically, produced over 200,000 lines of Lean code for Maryna Viazovska's 24-dimensional proof in two weeks, without a preexisting blueprint. The work follows the 8-dimensional formalization announced on February 23, which built on the Formalising Sphere Packing in Lean project.

⊢Checked against: spectrum.ieee.org

AI and formalization·Jan 20

Project Numina announces Numina-Lean-Agent, an open-source theorem-proving framework

Yann Fleureau announced Numina-Lean-Agent, an open-source agentic framework for autoformalization and theorem proving using generic AI models. The announcement reports 12/12 on Putnam 2025 and a formalization of the "Effective Brascamp-Lieb inequalities" research paper, more than 90% of it AI-generated.

AI and formalization·Jan 11

Axiom's AxiomProver solves all 12 Putnam problems with Lean proofs

AxiomProver, an autonomous AI theorem prover built by Axiom, solved 12 out of 12 problems from the Putnam Competition, 8 of them within the strict exam time limit, producing formal proofs in Lean. One combinatorial step in Problem A5 required 2,054 lines of formal proof.

⊢Checked against: LinkedIn post
