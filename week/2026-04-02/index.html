Lean core·Mar 27

Lean 4.29.0 released with a new extensible do elaborator

The release changes reducibility handling: isDefEq no longer bumps transparency when comparing implicit arguments, and a new @[implicit_reducible] attribute and a backward-compatibility option ease migration. It also adds grind support for higher-order Miller patterns, mvcgen support for specifications in the local context, and faster startup.

Verification·Mar 26

Bas Spitters releases CatCrypt, machine-checked cryptographic security proofs in Lean

CatCrypt provides a pipeline from Rust reference implementations, translated with the Hax tool, to security proofs in Lean, covering 172 cryptographic protocols and constructions (110 with the full Rust-to-Lean pipeline). Bas Spitters developed the whole library in two months working with GenAI.

Verification·Mar 18

Ilya Sergey proves Move's new borrow checker correct in Lean

Ilya Sergey formalised and proved the correctness of the new borrow checker for Move, a smart contract language deployed on the Sui and Aptos blockchains: 39,000 lines of mechanised metatheory, produced in under a month with the help of an AI coding assistant (Claude Code).

AI and formalization·Mar 9

Joseph Tooby-Smith's Lean formalization finds error in widely cited 2006 physics paper

Formalizing a 2006 paper on the stability of the two Higgs doublet model (2HDM) potential, using Mathlib and Physlib, revealed an error invalidating its main theorem. The paper calls this, to the best of its knowledge, the first non-trivial error in a physics paper found through formalization.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2603.08139

Lean core·Apr 2

leanprover-community releases a tool for offline Lean 4 teaching bundles

The bundle tool creates self-contained Lean 4 bundles for teaching: students download a zip, unpack it, double-click "Start_Lean", and get a working editor with no installation, no network access, and no command line needed. Each bundle ships VSCodium with the lean4 extension and only the oleans the project needs.

Papers and events·Apr 2

Leo de Moura publishes blog post "Why Lean?"

In a post dated 2026-04-02, Leo de Moura argues that Lean's implementation in Lean itself underpins its extensibility, scalability, and soundness through a small trusted kernel. The post also names backward compatibility as Lean's weakness: code written two years ago probably does not compile today without changes.
