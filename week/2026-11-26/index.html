Lean core·Nov 21

Joachim Breitner removes large elimination for recursive Prop inductives from Lean's kernel

The merged pull request, part of the Lean Kernel Renovation, restricts recursors such as Acc.rec to motives in Prop, while Eq and And keep large elimination. The goal is a kernel that never has to look at a proof term, which would let tools like external checkers drop proof subterms after typechecking.

⊢Checked against: lean4#13851

Verification·Nov 21

CertiK and Columbia University researchers formally verify GPU kernels in Lean

Using TensorLean, which checks compiled PTX code, the researchers proved 83 of 149 kernel instances conform to their Lean specifications, in what CertiK calls the world's first formal verification of production-grade AI operators. The team found 38 correctness issues across 6 libraries, including 2 in DeepSeek's DeepGEMM and 23 in the FlashAttention operator library.

⊢Checked against: panewslab.com

Jz Pan and contributors formalize Iwasawa's classical class-number growth theorem

The theorem describes how the p-adic multiplicity of class numbers grows along an increasing tower of finite cyclic Galois extensions of a number field. The Lean statement uses only Mathlib concepts, and the proof relies on Naganori Yamaguchi's external Nyll class field theory library for the Hilbert class field inputs.

AI and formalization·Nov 20

Harry Wang formalizes the Four Color Theorem in Lean 4 without Mathlib

The self-contained proof follows Georges Gonthier's hypermap framework and the published mathematics of Neil Robertson, Daniel P. Sanders, Paul Seymour, and Robin Thomas. It verifies all 633 unavoidable configurations (249 D-reducible, 384 C-reducible) and all 5 cartwheel discharging trees directly inside the Lean kernel without native_decide.

AI and formalization·Nov 19

AlphaProof Nexus finds Lean proof of high-dimensional photonic GHZ no-go theorem

Mario Krenn and coauthors used AlphaProof Nexus, which combines language models with Lean compiler feedback, to prove a Formal Conjectures statement: no complex-weighted monochromatic quantum graph exists for any even N of at least 4 when D is at least N. Generating an N-photon, D-dimensional GHZ state is equivalent to constructing such a graph.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2611.26300·paperswithlean.com

AI and formalization·Nov 16

Patrick Hayden machine-checks 3D passive quantum memory theorems in Lean

Whether 3D quantum systems with local interactions can passively protect quantum information has been open since Kitaev introduced topological quantum computation. Patrick Hayden's two-part paper shows a variant of Balasubramanian, Davydova, and Lin's code family resists thermal noise and weak quantum perturbations; Lean proofs of existential theorems for both parts have zero sorry placeholders.

Verification·Nov 19

Tether monitors AI agents with security policies written in Lean 4

Tether, by Meghana Aparna Sistla and coauthors, sandboxes proposed commands and commits side effects only if a policy allows them; its Lean 4 DSL is both the executable monitor and the specification for mechanized proofs. On coding-agent benchmarks, one shared policy blocks 100% of in-scope attacks with a 4.7% benign false-positive rate.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2611.25599·paperswithlean.com

Verification·Nov 18

Long Qian and André Platzer check ODE reachability proofs with Lean 4

The paper's differential dynamic logic proofs certify ordinary differential equation (ODE) reachability, like UNSAT proofs in satisfiability solving, and any approximation correctly computed via Taylor models (a technique for rigorous ODE bounds), even by untrusted algorithms, provably yields one. The checker, implemented in Lean 4 for improved performance, can formally verify ARCH competition benchmarks.

Papers and events·Nov 15

Kevin Schlegel posts a solution to the Birkhoff Problem on arXiv

For a commutative uniserial ring, the paper describes the indecomposable objects in its monomorphism category, independently of the specific ring for lengths up to 5 and depending only on the residue field for length 6. Classifying the monomorphism category of the integers modulo p⁶ thus reduces to a known case, resolving the Birkhoff Problem.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2611.20099
