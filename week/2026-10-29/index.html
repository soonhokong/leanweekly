AI and formalization·Oct 24

Wolfram Institute publishes reference Lean environment for Rule 30 Prize submissions

The verifier checks every submission against exactly this Lake project (Lean 4.35.0, Mathlib's v4.35.0 tag), and the submissions site has instructions for AI agents. The kit contains the official statements of Problems 1 and 2, proved partial results, 163 equivalences and 49 sufficient conditions for Problem 1, and 14 equivalent forms of Problem 2.

AI and formalization·Oct 23

Pranava Kumar registers a Lean proof of Nivat's conjecture with Palomar

The entry formalizes Pranava Kumar's manuscript, proving that any configuration on the integer plane over a finite alphabet with at most k times n distinct k by n patterns has a nonzero global period. Its independent Challenge states the full conjecture using only standard Mathlib notions; the 339-file proof needs only Lean's foundational axioms.

⊢Checked against: palomar-registry.org·PranavaKCode/nivat-lean

AI and formalization·Oct 23

Ziqing Xiang formalizes Haemers' conjecture for the generalized spectrum in Lean

The Mathlib-based formalization contains the accompanying paper's whole proof that the proportion of n-vertex graphs determined by their generalized spectrum (the spectra of the adjacency matrices of a graph and its complement) tends to 1 as n grows. It also proves the results the paper quotes from the literature, including Kneser's addition theorem.

⊢Checked against: palomar-registry.org·ziqingxiang/dgs

AI and formalization·Oct 23

Haruhisa Enomoto formalizes two classical theorems of Bautista, Gabriel, Roiter and Salmerón

Built on Mathlib, the complete formalization follows Haruhisa Enomoto's new proof that representation-finite algebras over algebraically closed fields have normed multiplicative bases (closed under products up to zero) and basic ones in characteristic other than two are standard (indecomposable modules match their Auslander–Reiten quiver's mesh category). It also proves the Auslander–Reiten theory it uses.

⊢Checked against: palomar-registry.org·haruhisa-enomoto/multiplicative-bases-deformations

Papers and events·Oct 22

Alexander Meiburg disproves perfect randomized composition for total functions, checked in Lean

Perfect randomized composition (a composition's randomized query complexity is always at least a constant times the product of its factors' complexities) was known to fail for partial Boolean functions but open for total ones. The main result is machine-checked in Lean 4 as a little-o statement on a subsequence of the construction.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2610.31638·paperswithlean.com

Verification·Oct 21

AssureCode uses Lean for structured justification of AI-generated code

Logan Murphy and coauthors describe AssureCode, which autoformalizes developer intent into Lean specifications for human approval, then decomposes claims via Lean templates, with Lean checking an agent's decomposition-soundness proofs. On HumanEval, LiveCodeBench and MBPP samples with four LLMs, a single run recovered most of an iterative LLM baseline's concerns and surfaced additional ones.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2610.29758·paperswithlean.com

Papers and events·Oct 20

Carlo Ciliberto, Alessandro Rudi, Enrico Tassi introduce Proof-Carrying Contracts for AI agents

An execution system called the Airlock runs a tool call only if the Lean kernel accepts the agent's proof that the call preserves the agreed constraints in every case its model allows. A Lean 4 mechanization proves the constraints hold at every instant of execution, even for an adversarial agent, under stated assumptions.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2610.28484·consequential-ai/proof-carrying-contracts·paperswithlean.com

Verification·Oct 19

Aditya Ranjan Jha and coauthors mechanize a hardware type system in Lean

Pact, a typed extension of the Anvil hardware description language, checks each component against its type, and compatible types guarantee communication safety (no unsynchronized exchanges or loss of progress). The POPL 2027 paper's artifact includes a Lean 4 mechanization proving soundness of type inference, type checking, and compatibility, plus optimality of inferred types.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2610.26599·doi.org

Verification·Oct 17

Anirudh Gupta, Siddhartha Gadgil and Petros Markopoulos present PastaLean for verifying Python

PastaLean deterministically translates Python into Lean through Python's abstract syntax tree, with no LLM, turning specifications written in Python into theorems for agents or humans to prove. It also includes TypeInfer, a Python type inference engine that completed one benchmark in 1.25 s against HeaderGen's 36.2 s, and an MCP server for coding agents.

⊢Checked against: anirudhg07.github.io·siddhartha-gadgil/PastaLean·leanprover.zulipchat.com

Verification·Oct 24

Anand Kumar Keshavan announces lean-qverify for verifying quantum circuits in Lean 4

The open-source project includes a Lean library, a Qiskit integration, and a free workbook with executable examples covering circuit specifications, semantic proofs, Quantum Hoare Logic and ZX-calculus. Anand Kumar Keshavan calls the current implementation an initial research and educational framework, welcomes independent technical reviews, and is interested in collaborating on compositional proof techniques.

Verification·Oct 23

Giulio rebuffo's Janus verifies compiled Go arm64 machine code in Lean 4

Janus, an early work in progress, decodes instructions in Lean and does not trust the Go compiler, linker, or any source-level reasoning. Every load and store must fall inside a caller-provided allocation, and Go runtime panic stubs (bounds checks, nil, divide) are named outcomes, so "this function never panics" is provable.

⊢Checked against: Giulio2002/Janus
