Google DeepMind uses AI agents to verify Signal's messaging security in Lean

The researchers used AlphaProof Code, a multi-agent framework for formal software specification and verification, on Signal's post-quantum PQXDH handshake and Triple Ratchet. The agents generated 6.5 million lines of Lean code proving the libsignal implementation correct against a human-reviewed specification; the proof assumes perfect cryptography.

Christian Merten and collaborators formalize the Weil conjectures with AI assistance

AI agents (Anthropic Claude models) wrote most of the proof under the authors' supervision, and the team spent about 80,000 USD on model access and server compute. Physical Superintelligence releases an AI-produced Lean proof of the Aaronson-Ambainis conjecture

The conjecture says a bounded polynomial of low degree on the discrete cube has an influential variable. AI models produced the proof, paper and formalization in a research harness developed by David Turturean at PSI; the formalization has no placeholder or added axiom and was re-checked by two independent Lean kernel implementations.

AI and formalization·Nov 9

ChatGPT Pro and Claude Opus 5.5 prove the Reinhardt conjecture in Lean

The conjecture, open since 1934, says the smoothed octagon (a regular octagon with its corners cut off by hyperbola arcs) packs the plane worst among centrally symmetric convex shapes. The-Anh Vu-Le directed the work, which follows Thomas Hales's optimal control formulation; no person has checked the proof, though Lean's kernel checks every step.

⊢Checked against: vltanh/lean4-smoothed-octagon·palomar-registry.org

Project Numina formalizes a braided fusion category preprint in Lean 4

The preprint, by Justin Asher, Jonah Berean-Dutcher, Clay Cordova, Julio Parra-Martinez and Richard Tuddenham, proves that every non-degenerate pivotal braided fusion category with property F is pseudo-unitary and weakly integral, assuming neither sphericality nor pseudo-unitarity. AI and formalization·Nov 3

Antony Lewis proves the Higuchi-Sudbery entanglement conjecture, with a Lean formalization

Among four-qubit pure states, only M₄ and its complex conjugate, up to local unitaries, maximize the mean von Neumann entropy of the three balanced bipartitions; the paper also proves the Gour-Wallach conjecture for Rényi entropies. The Lean 4 and Mathlib formalization covers most four-qubit results and parts of the computer-assisted seven-qubit ones.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2611.05757·cmbant/qubit-entropy

AI and formalization·Nov 7

Murali Menon proves Herzog-Schönheim conjecture for solvable groups, with Lean formalization

The Herzog-Schönheim conjecture states that if a group is partitioned into finitely many, and at least two, cosets of subgroups, then two of these subgroups have the same index. The arXiv paper proves it for all solvable groups, finite or infinite, and the proof has been formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2611.11002

AI and formalization·Nov 8

Carlos Toledo formalizes a seven-point counterexample to Graham's spherical-set conjecture

Dömötör Pálvölgyi's heptagon and generic seven points on a circle (Theorem A.3 of arXiv:2609.23327) are proved non-Ramsey under formal-conjectures' definition, copied byte for byte (Ramsey: for any number of colours, every colouring of some Euclidean space has a monochromatic copy). Anthropic's Claude wrote the Lean proofs under Carlos Toledo's direction, building on OpenAI's formalization.

AI and formalization·Nov 10

The-Anh Vu-Le registers Lean proofs of Brouwer's, Kakutani's and Schauder's fixed-point theorems

Brouwer's theorem, in every finite dimension, has two proofs: an analytic one, following John Milnor (1978) and C. A. Claude Opus 5.5 wrote the proofs in Claude Code; no person has reviewed them, and Lean has checked them.

⊢Checked against: palomar-registry.org·vltanh/lean4-fixed-point

Verification·Nov 10

AI-written Lean model of CPython 3.15.2 finds hundreds of bugs

David Andrews and Chao Zhang's model spans 613,318 lines of Lean with 18,898 theorems and lemmas; each modelled function must agree with the real interpreter on at least 50,000 cases. Of 26,004 functions in scope, 4,071 (15.7%) are judged faithful by independent review, and 418 bug records reproduce on 3.15.2, each with a reproducer.

⊢Checked against: Broyojo/python-formalization

Verification·Nov 8

Yalu Cai, Tung-Che Chang, and Santosh Nagarakatte verify an eBPF proof checker

Keel, an in-kernel proof checker for the Linux eBPF verifier, is specified in Lean and proved sound against bit-vector semantics; its implementation in EmbC, a deeply embedded C fragment, is proved to refine that specification. Formalizing the eBPF Certificate Framework (BCF) checker uncovered 13 bugs, and Keel runs 1.3X faster than BCF's checker.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2611.11117

Verification·Nov 7

Andreas Andreakis extends DBLog to delete orphan rows, with Lean 4 proofs

DBLog-based incremental snapshots, adopted by Debezium and Flink CDC, originally did not handle orphan rows (in the sink, absent at the source); the extension deletes keys a chunk read did not return. Safety and convergence are proved in Isabelle/HOL and, except for the Flink CDC theory, in Lean 4, with no unfinished proofs.

Jason Hickey proposes agent-authored software where humans review only formal specifications

Continuous integration checks replace code review: on a zero-copy authenticated message router, the kernel check passed in 42 ms and mutation testing killed all 10 synthetic bugs. The paper builds on Lean 4 artifacts (salt, x86lean, vir) and contrasts Lean subtypes with Computational Type Theory subset types; several components remain proposals.

Papers and events·Nov 3

Paper determines order of magnitude of low moments of random multiplicative sums

Christopher Atherfold, Maxim Gerspach and Jad Hamdan study the 2q-th moments, for q from 0 to 1, of sums up to x of a random, completely multiplicative function with independent, uniform ±1 values at the primes. A transition from better-than-square-root cancellation to growth like a power of log x occurs when q crosses 1/2.

⊢Checked against: arXiv:2611.05492
