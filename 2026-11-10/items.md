## Headlines

- **Google DeepMind uses AI agents to verify Signal's messaging security in Lean.** The researchers used AlphaProof Code, a multi-agent framework for formal software specification and verification, on Signal's post-quantum PQXDH handshake and Triple Ratchet. The agents generated 6.5 million lines of Lean code proving the libsignal implementation correct against a human-reviewed specification; the proof assumes perfect cryptography. ([news.lavx.hu](https://news.lavx.hu/article/google-deepmind-uses-ai-agents-to-verify-signal-s-secure-messaging-techniques))
- **Christian Merten and collaborators formalize the Weil conjectures with AI assistance.** AI agents (Anthropic Claude models) wrote most of the proof under the authors' supervision, and the team spent about 80,000 USD on model access and server compute. The Lean proof certifies the conjectures as stated in LeanEval; a blueprint website presents it as layered natural-language notes, unfinished in parts not directly needed for it. ([leanprover.zulipchat.com](https://leanprover.zulipchat.com/#narrow/channel/579630-Project-announcements/topic/Formalised.20notes.20of.20the.20Weil.20conjectures/with/638125972), [formal-arithmetic-geometry/weil-conjectures](https://github.com/formal-arithmetic-geometry/weil-conjectures), [weil-conjectures.merten.dev](https://weil-conjectures.merten.dev/), [axiommath.ai](https://axiommath.ai/research/the-weil-conjectures-formalized-in-lean/))
- **Physical Superintelligence releases an AI-produced Lean proof of the Aaronson-Ambainis conjecture.** The conjecture says a bounded polynomial of low degree on the discrete cube has an influential variable. AI models produced the proof, paper and formalization in a research harness developed by David Turturean at PSI; the formalization has no placeholder or added axiom and was re-checked by two independent Lean kernel implementations. ([physical-superintelligence/aaronson-ambainis](https://github.com/physical-superintelligence/aaronson-ambainis))
- **ChatGPT Pro and Claude Opus 5.5 prove the Reinhardt conjecture in Lean.** The conjecture, open since 1934, says the smoothed octagon (a regular octagon with its corners cut off by hyperbola arcs) packs the plane worst among centrally symmetric convex shapes. The-Anh Vu-Le directed the work, which follows Thomas Hales's optimal control formulation; no person has checked the proof, though Lean's kernel checks every step. ([vltanh/lean4-smoothed-octagon](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-smoothed-octagon), [palomar-registry.org](https://palomar-registry.org/entry?id=PALOMAR-2026-11-09-000008&version=1))
- **Axiom Math formalizes all four Weil conjectures in Lean.** The library, Weil, has about 220,000 lines of code, written by AI agents and not yet reviewed by humans; reusing Axiom Math's earlier Lean formalization of the Stacks Project, an algebraic geometry reference, let the team finish in 24 hours. The top-level theorem copies verbatim the LeanEval problem weil_conjectures, which uses only Mathlib definitions. ([axiommath.ai](https://axiommath.ai/research/the-weil-conjectures-formalized-in-lean/), [AxiomMath/Weil](https://github.com/AxiomMath/Weil), [leanprover.zulipchat.com](https://leanprover.zulipchat.com/#narrow/channel/579630-Project-announcements/topic/Weil.20conjectures/with/638126307))

## AI and formalization

- **Project Numina formalizes a braided fusion category preprint in Lean 4.** The preprint, by Justin Asher, Jonah Berean-Dutcher, Clay Cordova, Julio Parra-Martinez and Richard Tuddenham, proves that every non-degenerate pivotal braided fusion category with property F is pseudo-unitary and weakly integral, assuming neither sphericality nor pseudo-unitarity. The formalization also includes the extension to degenerate categories under a positivity hypothesis. ([project-numina/mtc](https://github.com/project-numina/mtc), [arXiv:2611.12836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12836))
- **Antony Lewis proves the Higuchi-Sudbery entanglement conjecture, with a Lean formalization.** Among four-qubit pure states, only M₄ and its complex conjugate, up to local unitaries, maximize the mean von Neumann entropy of the three balanced bipartitions; the paper also proves the Gour-Wallach conjecture for Rényi entropies. The Lean 4 and Mathlib formalization covers most four-qubit results and parts of the computer-assisted seven-qubit ones. ([arXiv:2611.05757](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05757), [cmbant/qubit-entropy](https://github.com/cmbant/qubit-entropy))
- **Murali Menon proves Herzog-Schönheim conjecture for solvable groups, with Lean formalization.** The Herzog-Schönheim conjecture states that if a group is partitioned into finitely many, and at least two, cosets of subgroups, then two of these subgroups have the same index. The arXiv paper proves it for all solvable groups, finite or infinite, and the proof has been formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib. ([arXiv:2611.11002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11002))
- **Carlos Toledo formalizes a seven-point counterexample to Graham's spherical-set conjecture.** Dömötör Pálvölgyi's heptagon and generic seven points on a circle (Theorem A.3 of arXiv:2609.23327) are proved non-Ramsey under formal-conjectures' definition, copied byte for byte (Ramsey: for any number of colours, every colouring of some Euclidean space has a monochromatic copy). Anthropic's Claude wrote the Lean proofs under Carlos Toledo's direction, building on OpenAI's formalization. ([palomar-registry.org](https://palomar-registry.org/entry?id=PALOMAR-2026-11-08-000001&version=1), [carlostoledo1891/heptagon-ramsey-lean](https://github.com/carlostoledo1891/heptagon-ramsey-lean))
- **The-Anh Vu-Le registers Lean proofs of Brouwer's, Kakutani's and Schauder's fixed-point theorems.** Brouwer's theorem, in every finite dimension, has two proofs: an analytic one, following John Milnor (1978) and C. A. Rogers (1980) and using no homology, degree theory or simplicial approximation, and one through Sperner's lemma. Claude Opus 5.5 wrote the proofs in Claude Code; no person has reviewed them, and Lean has checked them. ([palomar-registry.org](https://palomar-registry.org/entry?id=PALOMAR-2026-11-10-000001&version=1), [vltanh/lean4-fixed-point](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-fixed-point))

## Verification in practice

- **Veil 2.0 released for verifying distributed protocols in Lean 4.** The release adds verified model checkers for safety (2 to 20 times faster than TLC) and liveness, an LTL proof mode, a TLA+-to-Veil LLM skill, and a PlusCal-style DSL. George Pîrlea says Veil now supports anything the TLA+ Toolbox is used for; Ilya Sergey credits @zqy1018 and external contributors. ([@ilyasergey on X](https://x.com/ilyasergey/status/2118797820698521933), [@GeorgePirlea on X](https://x.com/GeorgePirlea/status/2118894026858566070))
- **AI-written Lean model of CPython 3.15.2 finds hundreds of bugs.** David Andrews and Chao Zhang's model spans 613,318 lines of Lean with 18,898 theorems and lemmas; each modelled function must agree with the real interpreter on at least 50,000 cases. Of 26,004 functions in scope, 4,071 (15.7%) are judged faithful by independent review, and 418 bug records reproduce on 3.15.2, each with a reproducer. ([Broyojo/python-formalization](https://github.com/Broyojo/python-formalization))
- **Yalu Cai, Tung-Che Chang, and Santosh Nagarakatte verify an eBPF proof checker.** Keel, an in-kernel proof checker for the Linux eBPF verifier, is specified in Lean and proved sound against bit-vector semantics; its implementation in EmbC, a deeply embedded C fragment, is proved to refine that specification. Formalizing the eBPF Certificate Framework (BCF) checker uncovered 13 bugs, and Keel runs 1.3X faster than BCF's checker. ([arXiv:2611.11117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11117))
- **Giulio rebuffo's usdt-verify formally verifies deployed USDT bytecode in Lean 4.** Built on EthLib's EVM model and program logic, the proofs are about the 11,075 deployed bytes read from Ethereum mainnet; the Solidity source served only as a guide. The README says each of its claims is proved and has passed several independent reviews, and that this does not mean USDT is verified unconditionally. ([Giulio2002/usdt-verify](https://github.com/Giulio2002/usdt-verify))
- **zkSecurity publishes Rust BLS12-381 code proved equivalent to the EthLib Lean specification.** The code covers field arithmetic, G1 encoding and decoding, and subgroup and pairing-product checks, specializing routines from the arkworks 0.5.0 crate; proofs establish termination and agreement with EthLib under documented preconditions, while arkworks agreement is tested and the Charon/Aeneas translation remains trusted. The project was mostly AI-generated and has not been independently reviewed. ([zksecurity/arkworks-ethlib](https://github.com/zksecurity/arkworks-ethlib))
- **Rudi Cilibrasi publishes openverse, an unofficial Verse runtime with Lean verification.** The project defines and proves the semantics of Verse (Epic Games' functional logic programming language) in Lean 4 and checks a C runtime against it. Its C components for integer arithmetic, program loading, and rollback are proved with QuickProof (VST and CompCert in Coq); differential testing and fuzzing check the object store and value heap. ([rudi-cilibrasi/openverse](https://github.com/rudi-cilibrasi/openverse))
- **Markus de Medeiros publishes SampLib, a Lean library of verified random samplers.** SampLib expresses samplers from Peter Occil's articles as executable ProbLang terms and proves them correct in continuous TotalEris, building on logsem/clutch-lean and Lean 4.35.0. Its completed baseline suite contains 216 samplers, and the README notes that the interpreter tests are not proofs and that the interpreter uses finite-precision Float arithmetic. ([markusdemedeiros/SampLib](https://github.com/markusdemedeiros/SampLib))
- **yaneurao publishes Lean 4 proofs for a shogi AI opening-book algorithm.** The algorithm, peta-shock conversion, propagates evaluation values recorded in a shogi AI's opening book backward from the end positions and covers books containing ordinary and perpetual-check repetitions. PaperClaims.lean links the accompanying paper's theorem numbers to Lean theorem names, and Audit.lean checks the axioms the theorems depend on. ([yaneurao/peta-shock](https://github.com/yaneurao/peta-shock))
- **Andreas Andreakis extends DBLog to delete orphan rows, with Lean 4 proofs.** DBLog-based incremental snapshots, adopted by Debezium and Flink CDC, originally did not handle orphan rows (in the sink, absent at the source); the extension deletes keys a chunk read did not return. Safety and convergence are proved in Isabelle/HOL and, except for the Flink CDC theory, in Lean 4, with no unfinished proofs. ([arXiv:2611.10810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10810), [doi.org](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24035424))

## Papers, events, press

- **Jason Rute says articles on Lean's "trust surface" should mention external checking.** In a post on X, Jason Rute wrote that writers covering Lean's "trust surface" without mentioning external checking, comparator, and con-leche have not done the homework, and that "there is a lot of false and misleading info out there." ([@JasonRute on X](https://x.com/JasonRute/status/2119669220485243369))
- **Jason Hickey proposes agent-authored software where humans review only formal specifications.** Continuous integration checks replace code review: on a zero-copy authenticated message router, the kernel check passed in 42 ms and mutation testing killed all 10 synthetic bugs. The paper builds on Lean 4 artifacts (salt, x86lean, vir) and contrasts Lean subtypes with Computational Type Theory subset types; several components remain proposals. ([arXiv:2611.02285](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02285), [paperswithlean.com](https://paperswithlean.com/paper/2611.02285.html), [jyh/vir](https://github.com/jyh/vir))
- **Paper determines order of magnitude of low moments of random multiplicative sums.** Christopher Atherfold, Maxim Gerspach and Jad Hamdan study the 2q-th moments, for q from 0 to 1, of sums up to x of a random, completely multiplicative function with independent, uniform ±1 values at the primes. A transition from better-than-square-root cancellation to growth like a power of log x occurs when q crosses 1/2. ([arXiv:2611.05492](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05492))
