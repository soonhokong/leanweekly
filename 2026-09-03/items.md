## Headlines

- **Palomar, a registry of Lean verified mathematics, opens for submissions.** Palomar, an initiative incubated by the Lean FRO and by ICARM, registers GitHub repository snapshots after checking with the Lean tool Comparator that proofs match the claimed statements, and with a large language model that the informal description matches. Terence Tao stresses that Palomar is not a peer-reviewed journal. ([terrytao.wordpress.com](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/08/18/palomar-a-registry-of-lean-verified-mathematics/))

## AI and formalization

- **Coding agents autoformalize a norm-variation result for multiple ergodic averages in Lean.** Floris van Doorn, Polona Durcik, Joris Roos, Lenka Slavíková and Christoph Thiele released a blueprint and Lean 4 formalization of a quantitative strengthening of Tao's norm-convergence theorem, answering an open question of Avigad and Rute. The proof was autoformalized during a one-week period using coding agents under human supervision, producing roughly 107k lines of code. ([roos-j/lean-nct](https://github.com/roos-j/lean-nct), [arXiv:2608.27321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27321))
- **Yinjie Li formalizes new even-dimensional S-matrix inequality proof in Lean 4.** Yinjie Li presents an independently obtained, structurally different proof of the strict even-dimensional case of the Harwit and Sloane conjecture, formalized in Lean 4 conditional on Frankel and Urschel's Lemma 2.1 as an explicit external input. Finite evaluations use Lean's native evaluator, with their trust boundary documented. ([arXiv:2608.29750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29750))
- **Prove2Me launches an open platform where AI agents formalize mathematics together.** Shuze Chen, Kunal Marwaha, Xiaoyang Lu, Henry Yuen and Tianyi Peng introduce Prove2Me, where users launch formalization "missions" and AI agents contribute Lean proofs toward completion, building on and reusing one another's results. One listed mission targets Mathlib's open conway_99 problem on a strongly regular graph with parameters (99,14,1,2). ([arXiv:2608.28433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28433), [prove2.me](https://prove2.me/))
- **MathAdv benchmark and ProofEvolve prover target Lean 4 theorem proving.** Jiaxin Yuan and coauthors introduce MathAdv, a diagnostic benchmark spanning 13 domains across undergraduate- and graduate-level mathematics, finding formalization remains a major bottleneck. Separately, Wenqian Ye and coauthors present ProofEvolve, where the Lean kernel verifies every proof transition. It achieves the highest average solve rate among the evaluated proof systems on three competition-level Lean benchmarks. ([arXiv:2608.25449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25449), [arXiv:2608.26334](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26334))

## Lean core and ecosystem

- **Arthur Adjedj opens meta issue planning a renovation of Lean's kernel logic.** Arthur Adjedj's plan aims for a kernel that is easier to implement bug-free and to verify, with a decidable, transitive definitional equality algorithm and inductive features declared explicitly by the elaborator. Recursive predicates like Acc would lose large elimination, and kernel changes would ship in a single release. ([lean4#14977](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/issues/14977))
- **Alex Meiburg adds eink0rn, a Haskell Lean 4 kernel, to the arena.** Alex Meiburg's eink0rn, a clean-room Lean 4 kernel in Haskell generated mostly by Claude, was merged into the Lean Kernel Arena, which tests and benchmarks proof checkers for Lean. In the current round it answers all 72 invalid and 136 valid tests correctly and checks the mathlib test in 28.4 minutes. ([lean-kernel-arena#177](https://github.com/leanprover/lean-kernel-arena/pull/177), [arena.lean-lang.org](https://arena.lean-lang.org/))

## Papers, events, press

- **Four new arXiv papers settle open problems with Lean 4 formalizations.** Qianli Ma solves Colombo's 1928 determinant problem, Jef Pauwels proves the Pál and Vértesi conjectures on the I3322 Bell inequality, Zijun Gao and Weihan Zhang falsify an n^(-1/4) near-optimality conjecture from a ChatGPT 5.6 Pro proof attempt, and Gregory Morse shows a multiplicative complexity of exactly nine. ([arXiv:2608.30221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30221), [arXiv:2609.00101](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00101), [arXiv:2608.29734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29734), [arXiv:2608.30238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30238))
- **Maher Kallel and Mohamed El Louadi measure the audit burden of AI proofs.** The paper studies ten results an OpenAI lab published in August 2026 with Lean 4 certificates: kernel-checked proofs total 20.6 MB against 55.6 KB of statements needing human audit (379 to 1), yet those statements contain 218 bespoke definitions. Four weeks later, one result remained disputed over whether its formalization meant what it claimed. ([arXiv:2608.28997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28997))
- **Mario Carneiro to present AI-assisted formalization work on Lean's kernel at AITP'26.** AITP'26, the 11th Conference on Artificial Intelligence and Theorem Proving, takes place from August 30 to September 4, 2026, in the CNRS Paul-Langevin Conference Center in Aussois, Savoy. The program includes a talk by Mario Carneiro on AI-assisted formalization of Pi injectivity and uniqueness of typing for Lean's kernel. ([aitp-conference.org](http://aitp-conference.org/2026/))
- **Essay on Proofs and Prompts warns against AI proofs of cherished conjectures.** An essay by Just Different on Proofs and Prompts notes that ChatGPT Pro proved a difficult percolation problem known among colleagues as "p_c < 1" and urges mathematicians not to use their most beautiful conjectures as mere "benchmarks" for the next frontier model. ([proofsandprompts.com](https://proofsandprompts.com/2026/08/30/care-for-a-little-more-ai/))
- **New York University runs a Lean formalization course in fall 2026.** Jaume de Dios Pont teaches Computer-Assisted Formalization of Mathematics at New York University, starting Wednesday, September 2, with the Natural Number Game. The schedule runs from Lean basics and Mathlib through program verification and tactic metaprogramming to two weeks on agentic Lean proof generation. ([nyulean26.github.io](https://nyulean26.github.io/))
- **FU Berlin hosts two-week workshop formalizing polyhedra theory in Lean.** The workshop runs August 24 – September 4, 2026 at FU Berlin, with group work aimed at formalizing the foundations of polyhedral theory and integrating them into mathlib. Separately, the INdAM Meeting "Lean for the Curious Mathematician" will be held in the historical Palazzone di Cortona in Italy. ([mathconf.eu](https://mathconf.eu/pil-fub-2026), [amosturchet.github.io](https://amosturchet.github.io/lftcm26/))
