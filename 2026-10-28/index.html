Anthropic's Claude generates Lean proof of subexponential deterministic factoring

Isaac Rudich and Louis-Martin Rousseau registered the proof that one deterministic Turing machine computes the smallest prime factor of every natural number N in time N^{o(1)}; the best previously published deterministic bounds known to the authors have exponent 1/5. The authors audited the statement and did not read the proof, which Lean checks.

AI and formalization·Oct 28

Scott Armstrong and Zohar Komargodski formalize the positive-measure KAM theorem in Lean

The headline theorem is proved: for a nearly-integrable Hamiltonian with a small enough perturbation, most of phase space is filled by invariant tori carrying quasi-periodic motion. Agents prove the frozen statements (approved line by line, never edited); 11 of 22 are proved so far, with 73,724 of an estimated 117,000 lines of Lean.

Verification·Oct 28

Open-source desktop OS ZENOS releases first preview with Lean-proved security decisions

ZENOS's services ask zenos-decide, built from the same Lean definitions as the proofs, whether a file opens, content may reach a printer or AI, and a send is allowed, treating missing or unreadable answers as refusals. The theorems rest only on Lean's standard axioms, but the unaudited preview is not for real sensitive data.
